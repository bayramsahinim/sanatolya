Why most Shorts die in the first 2 seconds

People don't scroll past a video because it looks bad. They scroll because nothing in the first sentence makes them need the next one.

Every script in this kit follows one rule: each line has to earn the next one. The hook opens a question, the middle keeps adding to it, and the payoff answers it, usually with a twist the viewer didn't see coming. A Short built like this gets watched to the end, and that's the number the algorithm cares about most.

The three formats

Format 1

The Paradox

One mind-bending idea, told calmly. It gets comments because everyone has an opinion.

45–60 s · ~18 lines

Format 2

The Countdown

"3 secret advantages of…" Fast, funny, and it gets sent to friends.

25–35 s · ~16 lines

Format 3

The Deadpan Tutorial

A painfully simple task explained like it's rocket science. Gets rewatched and shared.

15–20 s · ~7 lines

How to use this kit

  1. Pick a format that fits the mood you want: deep, funny, or dry.
  2. Pick a topic from that format's idea list, or use the topic-finding method on page 11.
  3. Fill in the template line by line. Don't skip any beats; each one has a job.
  4. Run it through the checklist on page 13 before you record.
  5. Record or generate the voice using the voice direction prompts on page 10.
Rule of thumb: read your script out loud with a timer. A Short that's 10 seconds too long almost always has one beat repeated twice. Find it and cut it.

Format 1

The Paradox

Take something everyone thinks is obvious and show them it isn't. The tone is calm and curious, like telling a friend a strange thought late at night.

  1. ObviousList 2–3 facts nobody would argue with. A quick rhythm, three items works best.
  2. Agree?Get the viewer to agree: "Everyone agrees, right?" They're now on your side.
  3. Crack"But here's something no one can prove." Lower your voice. This is the real hook.
  4. QuestionAsk the paradox as one simple question a 10-year-old would understand.
  5. Title"This is the ___ paradox." Naming it makes it feel real and easy to search.
  6. Explain3–5 short lines: where the idea comes from, using one everyday image.
  7. StakesMake it personal and a little unsettling: "Your blue could be my green."
  8. Twist"Here's the twist." Zoom out: the idea is bigger than it looked.
  9. Warm landingEnd on something comforting or beautiful. Paradoxes that end dark get fewer shares.
  10. CTAAsk a yes/no or "which one are you" question, then "Follow for a new paradox every day."
Why it works: beat 2 gets the viewer to agree, so beat 3 feels like it's happening to them. And the yes/no question at the end is easy to answer in one word, which is why paradox videos fill up with comments.

Format 2

The Countdown

"Being ___ has 3 secret advantages." Fast, cheeky, and made to be sent to a friend. The viewer should see someone they know in it.

  1. Hook"Being [group] has 3 secret advantages." A trait people are usually teased about works best.
  2. Number + title"One: the blanket." Name the situation in 2–3 words. Viewers instantly picture it.
  3. The othersShow the "normal" people struggling: "Tall people have to choose."
  4. The flipThe group wins: "Short people? They vanish under it."
  5. EscalatePush it one step too far: "They even plant a flag on top." This is the laugh.
  6. Repeat ×3Same rhythm for Two and Three. Make the third one the most absurd.
  7. Send CTA"Send this to your shortest friend." Sends count more than likes.

Format 3

The Deadpan Tutorial

A tutorial for something embarrassingly simple, read completely seriously. The joke lives in one single step that's a little too true.

  1. Title"How to [simple task]." A clear tutorial heading. No joke yet.
  2. Steps 1–4Obvious, literal instructions, read flatly. Each one builds up the setup.
  3. The stepOne step that calls out a real bad habit: "Do this within the same week." This is the whole video.
  4. MedalOver-the-top sincere congratulations: "Congratulations, you have texted back."

Real script: "How to Text Back" (19 s)

TitleHow to text back.A tutorial heading, straight-faced.
1–4Step one: Pick up your phone. / Step two: Open the message. / Step three: Type some words. / Step four: Press send.Patronising, flat, a pause after each "Step ___:".
The stepStep five: Do this within the same week.A touch slower and more pointed. Everyone gets tagged here.
MedalCongratulations, you have texted back.Proud, like awarding a medal.
Why it's so short: deadpan dies if it drags. Seven lines, under 20 seconds, so it loops. A video that's rewatched on loop counts as more watch time than its length.

Voice

Directing the narrator

The same words can land or die depending on how they're read. Whether you record yourself or use an AI voice, give a director's note for the whole video and for each key line.

AI voice tools like Gemini TTS or ElevenLabs follow written directions surprisingly well. Paste the note above the script. If you record yourself, read the note before every take.

Base note (use for every video, so your channel always sounds the same)

Narrator of a witty Shorts channel. Voice: young adult, energetic but natural, clean and bright, medium-fast pace, lightly sarcastic and clever. Natural pauses and comic timing. Not deep, not booming, not a documentary voice, never shouting, no fake excitement.
FormatAdd to the base note
ParadoxCalm, curious, a sense of wonder, like telling a friend a mind-bending thought late at night. "But here's…" lower and intriguing. The question slow, gently mind-blown. "Here's the twist." a beat of drama. Pauses of about a third of a second between lines.
CountdownFast pace like a well-edited Short, a knowing smile in the voice. Each number a quick, upbeat title. Dilemmas read as tragic, wins read as smug, stats read deadpan, the last item like a movie trailer. Pauses of about a quarter of a second.
DeadpanA calm, slightly patronising instructional voice, completely straight-faced. Each "Step ___:" like a tutorial heading, short pause, then the instruction, flat. The joke step a touch slower and more pointed. The final line proud and over-the-top sincere.
Line-level notes are the secret. Don't just say "be funny." Pick the 3–4 lines that matter most and say exactly how each should sound: "'Cold feet… or cold shoulders.' as a tragic dilemma."

Never run out of ideas

The 10-minute topic method

90 topics will get you through three months of posting. After that, use this method to find more.

Where to look

Paradox
  • Wikipedia's "List of paradoxes" and "List of cognitive biases"
  • Questions that start with "Why do I…" in search autocomplete
  • Shower-thought style forums and threads
  • Psychology terms with a catchy name
Countdown
  • Anything people get teased about (height, names, habits)
  • Birth order, personality types, "the friend who…"
  • Your own comments: "do one about ___"
  • Flip an existing video to the opposite group
Deadpan Write down every small task you put off this week. Each one is a video. The bad habit you're guilty of is the joke step.

Score it before you write it

QuestionScore
Can I say the hook in one sentence a 10-year-old understands?0–2
Will the viewer think of a specific person (to send it to)?0–2
Is there a one-word answer people will argue about in the comments?0–2
Does it have a twist or punchline I can say in one line?0–2
Can it be shown visually (blanket, sky, phone…)?0–2

7 or more: make it this week. 4–6: rework the hook. Under 4: skip it.

Before you hit record

The pre-record checklist

  • The hook is in the first sentence, not the second.
  • The real hook (the "crack" or the flip) lands before second 5.
  • Every line is under ~15 words. Long lines get cut in half.
  • No two beats say the same thing.
  • There's one twist or punchline, and it's near the end.
  • The ending feels good (warm, funny, or proud), not dark.
  • Exactly one call to action, and it matches the format.
  • Read out loud with a timer: Paradox ≤ 60 s, Countdown ≤ 35 s, Deadpan ≤ 20 s.
  • Director's note written for the whole video plus 3–4 key lines.
  • Every line can be shown on screen as an image or action.
  • Captions are on screen. Most people watch on mute at first.
  • The first frame already shows something interesting (no logo intros).
