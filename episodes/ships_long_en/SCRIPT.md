# The Night the Ships Crossed a Hill — long-form script (draft v1)

Horizontal 16:9, ~7.5 min, English. Information first; the humour lives in the pictures (silent gags), not in the narration.
Look target: brighter than the Ships short (Y ≈ 76), darker than Empty Fort (Y ≈ 133) → **Y ≈ 100–105**.

Sources: Kritovoulos (*History of Mehmed the Conqueror*), Doukas, Nicolò Barbaro's siege diary, Steven Runciman, *The Fall of Constantinople 1453*.
Numbers that the sources disagree on are given as "about" / "some".

---

## 1. Hook (0:00–0:30)

**NARRATION**
> On the morning of April 23rd, 1453, the defenders of Constantinople looked down at the one stretch of water they thought was perfectly safe… and saw enemy warships floating in it. Dozens of them. The harbour was still locked. The great chain was still in place. Nobody had broken through. So how did the ships get in? They came over a hill.

**PICTURES / GAGS**
- Dawn, mist on the Golden Horn. A sleepy guard yawns, looks down, rubs his eyes, looks again.
- Pull back: the chain intact; ships *inside* it. Title card: **THE NIGHT THE SHIPS CROSSED A HILL**.

---

## 2. The city (0:30–1:30)

**NARRATION**
> In 1453, Constantinople was more than a city. For over a thousand years it had been the capital of the Eastern Roman Empire — the Byzantine Empire. It sat on a triangle of land, with water on two sides and the strongest walls of the medieval world on the third.
> The land walls, built in the fifth century, ran for about six kilometres: a moat, an outer wall, and a towering inner wall, with nearly two hundred towers. For a thousand years, no army had ever broken through them.
> But by 1453, the empire around the city was almost gone. What was left was a proud, half-empty capital… surrounded by the rising power of the Ottomans.

**PICTURES / GAGS**
- Map card: the triangle, Sea of Marmara, Golden Horn, land walls. Labels: **1,000+ YEARS**, **~6 KM**, **~200 TOWERS**.
- Cut-away of the triple wall; a tiny attacker bonks into it and falls over.
- The empire's map shrinks to just the city ("ALL THAT'S LEFT").

---

## 3. The young sultan (1:30–2:30)

**NARRATION**
> The Ottoman sultan was Mehmed the Second. He was just twenty-one years old, and he had one goal: to take the city that his ancestors had failed to take.
> He prepared for it like an engineer. He built a fortress on the Bosphorus to cut off help from the north. He hired a Hungarian cannon-maker named Orban, who cast a bronze bombard so huge it needed dozens of oxen to pull it.
> In early April, he arrived outside the walls with an army of perhaps eighty thousand men. Inside, the last emperor, Constantine the Eleventh, had fewer than eight thousand defenders, among them a band of Genoese soldiers led by a famous captain, Giovanni Giustiniani.

**PICTURES / GAGS**
- Mehmed studying plans by candlelight; **AGE: 21** tag.
- Rumeli Hisarı rises on the Bosphorus.
- The giant cannon; a long line of oxen straining; one ox looks back at the cannon and sighs.
- Two armies side by side as counters: **~80,000** vs **<8,000**.

---

## 4. The chain (2:30–3:30)

**NARRATION**
> The defenders knew where the weak point was. In 1204, crusaders had captured Constantinople not from the land, but from the harbour — the Golden Horn — where the sea walls were lower and weaker.
> So before the Ottomans arrived, the Byzantines stretched an enormous chain across the mouth of the Golden Horn, from the city to the tower of Galata on the other side. It floated on wooden logs, and behind it waited the Christian ships.
> As long as the chain held, the harbour walls didn't need many defenders. And every soldier saved there could fight on the land walls.

**PICTURES / GAGS**
- Flashback card **1204**: knights climbing the sea wall from ships.
- The chain being pulled across on floats; workers heave; one link lands on someone's foot.
- Map: chain line drawn in red across the Horn. Label **THE WEAK POINT — LOCKED**.

---

## 5. A humiliating defeat (3:30–4:15)

**NARRATION**
> Mehmed's cannons pounded the land walls day after day, but every night the defenders rebuilt the damage. His fleet attacked the chain, and failed.
> Then, on April 20th, four Christian ships carrying supplies sailed straight through the Ottoman navy in front of the whole city. The sultan was so furious that he rode his horse into the sea, shouting orders at his admiral. The four ships reached the chain, it was lowered for them, and they slipped into the harbour.
> The siege was two weeks old, and it was going badly.

**PICTURES / GAGS**
- Night repair: defenders patch the wall with barrels and earth.
- Four tall ships pushing through a swarm of small galleys.
- Mehmed on horseback, waist-deep in the sea, horse looking unimpressed.
- Chain lowers, ships slip in, chain goes back up.

---

## 6. The plan (4:15–5:00)

**NARRATION**
> So Mehmed decided that if his ships couldn't go through the chain, they would go around it — over land.
> Behind the Genoese colony of Galata rose a hill. On the other side, the Golden Horn — behind the chain. His men cleared a road over it, laid down wooden rails and rollers, and covered them with fat and oil.
> The idea wasn't completely new. Only fourteen years earlier, the Venetians had hauled a fleet over the mountains to Lake Garda in Italy. But no one expected it here, and certainly not this fast.

**PICTURES / GAGS**
- Map: dotted line from the Bosphorus shore over the hill to the Horn, chain still red. Label **AROUND IT**.
- Men laying logs; buckets of grease; a worker slips and slides down the rails.
- Small inset card: **1439 — LAKE GARDA**.

---

## 7. The night (5:00–6:00)

**NARRATION**
> On the night of April 22nd, the operation began. Ships were pulled from the Bosphorus onto cradles, and teams of oxen and men hauled them up the slope on the greased rollers.
> According to the chroniclers, the sailors sat at their oars and the sails were raised, as if the ships were already at sea, while drums and trumpets played. By morning, around seventy ships had crossed the hill and slid down into the Golden Horn.
> These weren't giant warships. Most were light galleys and smaller vessels. But they were enough.

**PICTURES / GAGS**
- The procession by torchlight: oxen, ropes, drummers, ships tilted uphill.
- Rowers pulling oars through the air; one rower looks down at the grass, confused.
- Counter in the corner ticking up: **1… 10… 30… 70 SHIPS**.
- Ships sliding down into the water at first light; splash.

---

## 8. What it changed (6:00–6:50)

**NARRATION**
> The chain had not been broken, but it no longer mattered. Now the defenders had to guard the long sea walls along the harbour, and pull men away from the land walls, where they were desperately needed.
> On April 28th, the Venetians tried to burn the new Ottoman fleet in a night raid. The Ottomans had been warned. The attack failed, and the lead ship was sunk.
> Soon the Ottomans built a floating bridge across the Golden Horn, letting troops and cannons reach the harbour walls. The city was now under pressure from almost every side.

**PICTURES / GAGS**
- Map: defender icons sliding away from the land walls to the harbour. Label **SPREAD THIN**.
- Night raid: small boats with fire, cannon flash, a ship goes down.
- Pontoon bridge of barrels and planks.

---

## 9. The end (6:50–7:30)

**NARRATION**
> Before dawn on May 29th, 1453, after nearly two months of siege, the Ottomans launched their final assault. Giustiniani was badly wounded, the defence broke, and the last Byzantine emperor died fighting at the walls.
> Constantinople fell. More than a thousand years of Roman rule in the east came to an end, and the city became the new Ottoman capital: Istanbul.
> The ships that crossed the hill didn't win the siege by themselves. But in a single night, they turned an impossible wall of water into just another front — and showed what a twenty-one-year-old sultan was willing to try.

**PICTURES / GAGS**
- Dawn assault, flags on the walls (no gore).
- Final wide shot: the Golden Horn at sunrise; a ship on the hill silhouette.
- End card: **1453 — THE END OF AN EMPIRE**.
