In 1943, a British admiral pulled out his pistol in front of Churchill's generals... and shot a block of ice.
You see, German submarines are sinking ships in the middle of the Atlantic, where no plane can reach.
And Britain is short of steel.
So an inventor named Geoffrey Pyke has a crazy idea. An aircraft carrier... made of ice. Two thousand feet long.
The generals laugh. "Ice? Ice sinks ships. It doesn't make them!"
But under a meat market in London, hidden behind frozen carcasses, scientists are freezing water with a secret ingredient.
Then Lord Mountbatten carries one grey block to Churchill... who is in his bath.
Splash. Into the hot water. And it doesn't melt.
Or so Mountbatten claimed.
Now, the big meeting in Quebec. Two blocks on the table.
He shoots the first one. Plain ice. It explodes into pieces.
He aims at the second one...
The bullet bounces right back, and buzzes around the generals' legs like an angry bee.
Nobody is laughing now.
The secret ingredient? Sawdust.
Wood fibers hold the ice together, and wet sawdust barely melts.
The ship was never built. But pieces of its test boat still lie at the bottom of a lake in Canada.
