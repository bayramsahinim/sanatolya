This man promised the Empress he'd move a rock the size of a house... and then he sat down and waited for snow.
You see, it's Russia, 1768. Catherine the Great wants a giant pedestal for her statue of Peter the Great.
And in a swamp outside Saint Petersburg, they find the perfect rock. Locals call it the Thunder Stone.
The problem? It's one of the heaviest stones humans have ever tried to move. And it's sitting in mud.
The engineers laugh. "It'll sink before it moves an inch."
Marinos Carburis builds a giant wooden sledge, and two long tracks with a groove down the middle.
And into the grooves... he drops bronze balls.
Balls? Under a mountain? Now the whole city is laughing.
Then winter comes. The swamp freezes solid. And the mountain... starts to roll.
Hundreds of men turn giant winches. Drummers keep the beat. Behind the stone, workers grab the track and run it to the front.
Stonemasons carve it while it's moving.
In January, the Empress comes to watch. The rock glides past her like a tray on marbles.
So why wait for snow?
Because a frozen swamp is a road. And a ball doesn't drag. It rolls.
Today, Peter the Great still rides on top of it.
