# Ships Over the Hill (English, ~57 s)

Same pattern as the reference Shorts: an unnamed hero does something strange in the first sentence,
somebody mocks him, the stakes rise, a "You see" line explains the trick, and the payoff lands on the last word.

## Source

The siege of Constantinople, April 1453: Kritovoulos 1.42, Doukas 38, Nicolò Barbaro's diary (22 April). The video never names the sultan.

| Beat | Sources |
|---|---|
| The Golden Horn is closed by a boom chain on floating logs; the besiegers' fleet fails to force it | yes |
| Supply ships (three Genoese and one imperial, 20 April) fight their way into the harbour | yes |
| Advisers urge lifting the siege | yes (Halil Pasha) |
| A road of greased wooden rollers over the hill behind Galata | yes |
| About seventy ships hauled overland in one night and launched in the Horn | yes (sources give 67–80; "seventy" is the round figure most use) |
| Crews at the oars, sails raised, drums and pipes playing during the haul | yes (Kritovoulos) |
| "Ships can't climb hills", "Does he think ships can walk?", the padlock on the chain | our gags |
