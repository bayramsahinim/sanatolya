To stop an army of a hundred and fifty thousand men, he opened the gates. And played a song. You see, his soldiers are far away. In this little town he has a few thousand men, mostly cooks and old guards. Then a scout runs in: the enemy's entire army is coming, and it's almost here. Run? Too slow. Fight? Impossible. His officers start to panic. So he gives the strangest orders of the war. Take down every flag. Hide every soldier. Open all the gates. And sweep the road, like guests are coming. Then he climbs the wall, lights some incense, sits down with his zither, and starts to play. Smiling. The enemy general rides up and freezes. Open gates. Empty streets. A man playing music. He knows this man. He never takes risks. Never. So this has to be a trap. And he turns the whole army around and runs. You see, a careful man doesn't bluff. So nobody believed he was bluffing. Behind those walls there was no army. Just cooks. And one very good actor.
