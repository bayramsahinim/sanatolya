## Facts and sources

| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| A great earthquake struck Pompeii in 62 (some date it 63) and the town was still being repaired in 79 | Seneca, *Naturales Quaestiones* VI.1; Tacitus, *Annals* XV.22; unfinished repairs seen in the excavations |
| The town was rebuilt in the same place | archaeology of the post-62 repairs |
| Vesuvius was green and covered in vineyards; nobody treated it as a volcano | Martial, *Epigrams* IV.44 ("here Vesuvius, green with vine shade"); Strabo only guessed at old fires |
| In 79 the ground shook for days; people were not alarmed "because it is common in Campania" | Pliny the Younger, *Letters* 6.20 |
| The eruption began around midday (the "seventh hour") | Pliny the Younger, *Letters* 6.16 |
| Pliny the Younger, 17, watched from Misenum across the bay; the cloud looked like an umbrella pine | *Letters* 6.16 |
| His uncle Pliny the Elder, commander of the fleet at Misenum, launched the warships to rescue people and died at Stabiae | *Letters* 6.16 |
| Pumice fell on Pompeii for many hours (no exact number stated); roofs collapsed under the weight | Sigurdsson et al. 1985; Luongo et al. 2003 |
| Many fled; others sheltered indoors | Luongo et al. 2003 (victims found in houses and streets) |
| Next morning pyroclastic surges reached the town, faster than a person can run | Sigurdsson et al. 1985 |
| Gas dissolved in magma comes out as pressure drops, like a shaken soda | standard volcanology |
| In 1599 Domenico Fontana's canal (Canale Conte di Sarno) ran under the buried city and inscriptions were found, but no one realized it was Pompeii | standard history of the excavations; systematic digging began in 1748 |

Uncertain numbers (population, victims, pumice depth, exact date of the eruption) are left out on purpose.
