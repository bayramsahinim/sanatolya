Pompeii got seventeen years of warnings, and spent them fixing the roofs.
You see, in the year 62, a huge earthquake wrecked the city. The plan? Rebuild. Same spot.
The mountain above them was green and covered in vineyards. To them, it was just a mountain.
Then, in 79, the ground shook for days. The locals shrugged: "Relax, it's Campania. It always shakes."
Around midday, the mountain exploded.
Across the bay, a teenager named Pliny watched a cloud shaped like a giant pine tree climb into the sky.
His uncle, an admiral, grabbed the fleet and sailed straight toward it, to rescue people. He never came back.
Over Pompeii, it started raining stones. Light pumice, for hours.
And those freshly fixed roofs? They caved in under the weight.
Many people ran. Others hid indoors, waiting for it to stop.
It didn't. The next morning, an avalanche of scorching gas and ash raced down the mountain, faster than anyone could run.
You see, magma is like a shaken soda. Gas builds pressure for centuries, then the cap pops, and everything comes out at once.
Pompeii vanished so completely that in 1599, an architect dug a tunnel right through it, and never realized.
Seventeen years fixing the roofs. One day to lose them.
