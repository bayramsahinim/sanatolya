This man tried to calm down violent prisoners... with a bucket of bubblegum pink paint.
You see, it's 1979. Scientist Alexander Schauss thinks colors can change how we feel.
He stares at a pink card... and his heart slows down.
So he walks into a Navy jail in Seattle, and asks the directors, Baker and Miller, to paint a cell pink.
Not just any cell. The room where angry new prisoners sit alone and cool off.
The guards laugh. "Pink? For these guys?"
But then the Navy report comes in. No hostile incidents. None.
The color gets a name: Baker-Miller Pink. Jails across America grab their paint rollers.
A football coach in Iowa even paints the visitors' locker room pink.
Then a jail in California tries it... and after the first month, the fights go up.
So in Switzerland, scientists finally test it properly. Fifty-nine angry prisoners. Half get a pink cell. Half get a white one.
Three days later... both groups are calmer. Exactly the same.
So what calmed them down?
Not the paint. Time. An angry man left alone in a quiet room cools off... whatever color the walls are.
But in Iowa, the visitors' locker room is still pink.
