# Fire Oxen (English, ~58 s)

Same pattern as the three reference Shorts: an unnamed hero does something strange in the first sentence,
somebody mocks him, the stakes rise, a "You see" line explains the trick, and the payoff lands on the last word.

## Source

Hannibal's escape from the Ager Falernus, autumn 217 BC: Livy 22.16–17 and Polybius 3.93–94. The video never names him.

| Beat | Sources |
|---|---|
| His army is boxed into a plain ringed by mountains; the pass is held by 4,000 Romans; Fabius waits for hunger | yes (Livy: 4,000 at the pass) |
| Dry branches/brushwood tied to the horns of about 2,000 cattle, lit at night, driven up the ridge by the pass | yes |
| The guards take the lights for the army escaping over the heights, leave the pass and chase them | yes |
| The army marches out through the empty pass | yes |
| At dawn the guards are among the cattle (and some light troops) | yes |
| "The fox is in the trap", the officer's barbecue line, the eyes in the bushes, the final "MOO." | our gags |

Sources say "oxen"/"cattle"; the narration says "cows" for the joke.
